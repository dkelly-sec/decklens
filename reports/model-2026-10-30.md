# DeckLens model report (2026-10-30)

Predicts which ranked deck wins a 1v1 battle from the two decks' cards, card levels and how highly each player was ranked at the time. Player identity is never used.

## Data

- Window: last 14 days
- Training: 12,997 battles, 2026-10-16 09:21 UTC to 2026-10-30 00:03 UTC
- Test: 3,251 battles, 2026-10-30 00:03 UTC to 2026-10-30 05:36 UTC (strictly later than all training battles)
- League filter: league 7+
- Cards modelled: 121 (seen at least 30 times in training)
- Skill data: both players ranked in 26.1% of battles, one player in 5.9%; the rest count as equally unranked
- Test battles won by side A: 47.3% (sides are assigned by tag order, so this should be near 50%)

## Results on the test set

| Model | Accuracy (95% range) | Log loss | Brier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coin flip (always 50%) | 50.0% (48.3%–51.7%) | 0.6931 | 0.2500 |
| Card levels only | 50.2% (48.5%–52.0%) | 0.6933 | 0.2501 |
| Player skill only | 50.2% (48.4%–51.9%) | 0.6931 | 0.2500 |
| Logistic regression (cards + levels + skill) | 51.2% (49.5%–52.9%) | 0.6930 | 0.2499 |
| Gradient-boosted trees (card pairs + levels + skill) | 53.0% (51.3%–54.7%) | 0.6898 | 0.2483 |

Lower log loss and Brier are better. A coin flip scores 0.6931 log loss and 0.25 Brier.

**Do decks matter beyond skill?** Yes: adding the decks to skill lowers log loss by 0.0001. (Logistic regression compared with the skill-only baseline.)

## Limits

- Rank only exists for the top 10,000; below that, players count as equally skilled.
- Card weights are average strength in the current meta, not counters; the tree model is the one that can learn card-vs-card interactions.
- Rare cards are left out until they've been seen enough times.
